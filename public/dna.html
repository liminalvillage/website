Our DNA

Just as biological DNA encodes how an organism grows, a community's social DNA is the code that shapes how it lives: why it exists, what it values, how its people relate, and what each person does every day. This page is that code, written down. If you come to Liminal Village, this is what you are joining and what we expect of you.

01 · Why we exist

Purpose

Liminal Village exists to prototype a regenerative civilization, by weaving together community living, healing practices and open-source technologies, and sharing everything we learn.

We are a living laboratory: a co-living, co-creation and eco-tech hub where temporary yet intentional communities practise, here and now, the future they want to see.

Extraction Regenerationin how we use land, energy and resources
Isolation Belongingin how we live, heal and care
Scarcity Shared abundancein how we own, exchange and decide
The problem

Extractive systems leave us disconnected

Modern society runs on economies that deplete resources, institutions that alienate people and technologies that speed up disconnection instead of deepening belonging. The result is a set of overlapping crises: ecological collapse, social fragmentation, spiritual disorientation and a loss of purpose.

Most responses are siloed, technocratic or transactional. They don't engage the whole human being or the interconnectedness of life. What is missing is a living example of how we might live differently.

Our answer

A real-world lab you can live in

We host temporary yet intentional communities where people from diverse backgrounds co-create new forms of economy, governance, education and wellness rooted in care, trust and interdependence.

Through holistic design, web3 infrastructure and ecological systems thinking, we make individual healing and collective transformation possible, and we document our tools and practices so other communities can do the same.

Vision

A network of regenerative communities

A thriving, interconnected network of regenerative communities, each contributing in its own way to a new global eco-civilization. Liminal Village is one beacon in that movement, and the community hub of the ReGenerativa network alongside ReFactory and Casa Selva.

Mission

Heal and transform, personally and collectively

Align personal, local and planetary needs. Invite people to co-design how we live, work, learn and care together. Shift society's foundation from scarcity to shared abundance, and from separation to conscious interdependence, or in our shorthand: upgrade the operating system of society.

03 · What we hold

Core values

Values are the compass for every decision, from what we build to who does the dishes. Each one below comes with what it looks like in practice, so you can tell whether we, and you, are living it.

Regeneration

Leave every system, whether soil, person, space or relationship, healthier than we found it. Nature is our primary teacher and we are part of it, not apart from it.

In practice: leave rooms and common spaces cleaner than you found them; compost, recycle, mind the water.

Interdependence

Everything is connected. Personal, local and global wellbeing are one system, and no one thrives alone.

In practice: contribute on all three levels: yourself, the village and the planet.

Care

Care for people, care for the earth, fair share. Care is work, and it counts: cooking, cleaning, childcare and emotional support hold the village together.

In practice: if you see a job that needs doing, it's yours. If others are busy, you probably should be too.

Trust & accountability

Trust is what lets us replace rules and money with agreements. It is built by keeping our word and kept alive by honest feedback.

In practice: make clear micro-agreements, keep them, and say so early when you can't.

Commons over ownership

Spaces, tools and knowledge are stewarded together, not hoarded. We work against the tragedy of the commons by making shared responsibility visible.

In practice: return what you borrow, follow the protocols, label only what is truly personal.

Gift & shared abundance

We move from scarcity to access abundance. A gift is given without expecting anything back, and that is how abundance spreads.

In practice: share food, skills and time freely; gift the community an experience during your stay.

Openness & transparency

Open-source technology, open finances, open learning. What we discover belongs to the network of communities that come after us.

In practice: all money flows are public on Open Collective; document what you build so others can reuse it.

Experimentation

We are a lab, not a utopia. We try things, measure, fail, learn and adapt. Tensions are information, not threats.

In practice: bring proposals rather than complaints; treat each lunation as an experiment to improve on.

Wholeness

We engage the whole human being: body, mind, emotions and spirit. Inner work and outer work are the same work.

In practice: take part in circles and feedback sessions, and own your parts, triggers and needs.

Living with natural cycles

We let the moon and the seasons, not the factory clock, set our rhythm. The lunar cycle is a planet-wide progress bar any community can sync to.

In practice: arrive with the new moon, review and celebrate at the full moon, close the cycle together.
04 · How we relate

Agreements

These are the shared grammar of community life. Everyone who joins agrees to practise them, imperfectly but sincerely.

Don Miguel Ruiz

The Four Agreements

  1. Be impeccable with your word. Speak with integrity and kindness, and only commit to what you will do.
  2. Don't take anything personally. Others' words and actions mostly reflect their own story.
  3. Don't make assumptions. Ask instead of guessing; clarify instead of concluding.
  4. Always do your best. Your best changes day to day; give it without self-judgment.
Marshall Rosenberg

Non-Violent Communication

  1. Observation. What happened, without judgment or interpretation.
  2. Feeling. What you feel in response to it.
  3. Need. The universal human need behind that feeling.
  4. Request. A clear, doable request, not a demand.
Holacracy, soft version

Tensions are opportunities

A tension is a gap between how things are and how they could be. Instead of letting it turn into gossip or resentment, bring it to the right place: to the person concerned (using NVC), to the role that owns the issue, or to the next circle or feedback session. Every tension you voice with a proposal makes the village better. Silent ones make it worse.

05 · What we do

Practices: what is expected of you

You are not a guest at a retreat. You become an active member of a temporary community, a co-creator responsible for its success. These are the practices everyone is expected to take on.

Personal

Set your own goals for the lunation. Find your ikigai, learn something new, write the book, give the talk.

Local

Do something for the people and land around you: teach, build, create art, regenerate the place.

Global

Pick a cause that matters for the planet, learn about it, and find a way to help.

Before you arrive

Align

  • Apply and read this page, the onboarding docs and the protocols.
  • Become a member of ReGenerativa APS (or join as a short-term visitor by donation).
  • Agree your micro-agreements: how you will contribute, drawing on any of the 8 forms of capital.
  • Check dates with the stewards before booking travel, then send exact arrival and departure times in writing.
  • Plan to arrive with the new moon and stay for a lunation (or a phase, about one week).
Every day

Contribute

  • Give your hours: at least 2 hours a day as a member, 4 as a volunteer.
  • Take on tasks and roles through the village bot, and follow through on what you claim.
  • Care for the commons: if you see a job that needs doing, it's yours.
  • Follow the protocols for kitchen, household, garden, tools and animals.
  • Small courtesies: no shoes in common spaces, label personal food, share meals as gifts.
Every lunation

Co-create

  • Gift an experience: a talk, workshop or practice for the community, in rotation.
  • Join circles and feedback sessions: we review how well we keep our agreements.
  • Voice tensions with proposals, using NVC and the right role or circle.
  • Propose projects that serve the village or the network, validated by the stewards or the community.
  • Take part in collective cleaning and shared moments like meals, fire and celebration.
Before you leave

Close the cycle

  • Give three days' notice of your departure so someone can help you leave.
  • Clean your room, wash or hand in your sheets, and wipe your yoga mat.
  • Return everything borrowed and take your garbage with you.
  • Leave it better: a regenerative deep clean earns you bonus points (and your deposit back).
  • Harvest the experience: send the stewards a 2–5 minute video about your stay.
What counts as contribution: maintenance, cleaning, cooking, gardening, childcare, supporting events and facilitation, emotional and relational support, offering workshops, and approved regenerative projects. What doesn't: attending social activities, circles or workshops on their own. A commitment you don't keep has a cost, because fairness is how the commons stays alive.
06 · How we organise

Roles & membership

We use a soft version of Holacracy. Authority sits in roles, not people. Roles have clear accountabilities, anyone can hold several, and roles change as the village's needs change. Circles coordinate the work, and stewards hold the long-term care of the place.

Village roles

Culinary Coordinator

Plans and prepares meals, manages the pantry, keeps the kitchen clean and food waste low.

Sustainability Coordinator

Tends the garden, supplies fresh produce, keeps resource use efficient.

Space & Logistics

Keeps communal spaces tidy and organised, manages supplies and logistics.

Community Facilitator

Leads meetings, mediates conflicts, upholds agreements, organises learning.

Wellness Coordinator

Offers wellness and healing practices, cultural activities and emotional support.

Technology & Finance

Runs the digital platforms, keeps the finances transparent, supports tech in daily life.

Ways to take part

There is no fixed room price. Contributions cover the real costs of the place, and stewards allocate spaces based on community needs, length of stay and the kind of contribution. Any surplus is reinvested. Exceptions are handled through transparent micro-agreements, and the whole system evolves through the association's circles and assemblies.
